# FishingFinds — an alternative to Beachcombing for every frozen lake

**Beachcombing only exists in four coastal regions. You can fish in twenty.**
With FishingFinds, a hole in the ice is more than a source of fish. Sometimes your line brings up something that sank long ago: a coil of fishing line, a can of food, an old jacket… and once in a long while, something worth spending the whole winter by that hole for.

Live where you want to live — not where the sea happens to wash things ashore.

## Just install and play

There is nothing to set up. Drop the mod into your `Mods` folder, sit down by a fishing hole, and the world beneath the ice is already waiting. The mod takes care of the rest.

## Hunting for finds is your choice

Want items? Drop an **empty hook** into the hole — no bait, no lure. That is the main way to find things: this is how useful items most often come up from the bottom.

Want food? Use bait. Fish bite as usual, but luck doesn't turn away completely: now and then, even a baited hook snags a find. And tip-ups, while you are busy with other things, sometimes bring back more than fish.

Every item you pull out is a fish you won't eat. Hunting for finds is not a free gift — it's a deliberate decision: are you getting food today, or looking for what you lack?

## Everything Beachcombing gave you — and more

Whatever the sea throws onto the coast, you can now bring up from any lake:

- old planks, firewood, sticks, scrap metal, cloth and leather;
- saplings, bark, lichen and other gifts of the forest carried off by the current;
- hooks, line and tackle lost by other fishermen, arrows of unlucky hunters;
- matches, flares, cartridges — everything for fire and protection;
- canned food, soda and supplies from someone's backpack;
- knives, hatchets and tools that fell through the ice;
- medicine — a rare but life-saving find;
- clothing and footwear — from wool socks to an expedition parka.

And a few things Beachcombing never gave: a cooking pot, an insulated flask, a storm lantern, trader clothing, a heavy hammer… and even a bundle of cash, which of course is of no use to anyone out here.

## Balance you can trust

This mod was not made to bury you in loot.

Every category was tuned separately. The chance of each of the 173 items was set by hand and checked on hundreds of catches in game and millions of rolls in calculations. Junk comes up often — just like in real life. Useful things turn up from time to time. And truly valuable finds stay rare enough to remember.

The better your ice fishing skill, the more often the ice shares its secrets — and the most valuable items open up only to an experienced fisherman. Levelling ice fishing matters again.

Playing Interloper or Misery? One checkbox in the settings, and the mod follows the same rules as the game: nothing that shouldn't exist on that difficulty.

But I don't recommend it. The balance is already built so that valuable finds stay rare and don't break the challenge. The checkbox doesn't make the game fairer — it just turns about every fifth find back into a fish and removes all the added items.

## For the curious

**How it works.** When you pull out a fish, the mod decides whether to replace it with an item:

| How you fish | Chance of an item instead of a fish |
|---|---|
| Empty hook, no bait or lure | from 25% at ice fishing level 1 to 45% at level 5 |
| With bait or a lure | 12.5% |
| Tip-up | 12.5%, without the rarest items |

Your current chance is shown right in the game, in the Ice Fishing skill description.
The full table with the chance of every item is in [docs/loot-table.md](docs/loot-table.md).

**Settings** — *Options → Mod Settings → Fishing Finds*, in English and Russian. You can turn off finds with bait or on tip-ups, switch on the Interloper/Misery mode, and remove or bring back individual items. For experienced players there are deep and extreme settings where you can change everything, down to the rarity of every single item. But the defaults are ready to play.

## Requirements

- The Long Dark 2.x (tested on 2.54)
- MelonLoader 0.7.x
- ModSettings
- *Tales from the Far Territory* DLC — optional. Without it, DLC items simply never come up.

## Installation and removal

Put `FishingFinds.dll` into the game's `Mods` folder.
To remove the mod, delete `FishingFinds.dll`, plus `FishingFinds.json` and `FishingFinds_Loot.json` from the same folder. The mod doesn't touch your saves.
